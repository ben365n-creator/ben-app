Why Concrete

The Foundation Is Everything

A pickleball court is only as good as what’s underneath it. An improper pour — wrong slope, wrong mix, wrong finish — leads to cracking, pooling water, and a surface that won’t hold a coating. We build the foundation properly so whatever surface you choose performs the way it should.

  • 📈
    2% Slope for Proper Drainage

    Water that pools on a court surface damages the coating and creates unsafe playing conditions. Every pad we pour is graded with a 2% drainage slope — water moves off quickly, every time.

  • 🧱
    4″–5″ Reinforced Slab

    4″ minimum, #4 rebar grid, 3,000–4,000 PSI mix. No shortcuts on thickness or reinforcement — this slab carries decades of use.

  • ✅
    Broom Finish Built for Acrylic

    Concrete for a pickleball court is not a driveway pour. The finish must bond correctly with the acrylic coating. We finish specifically for sport court applications.

  • 🕐
    28-Day Cure Before Surface Coat

    We respect the cure window. Coating too early causes delamination. We build the schedule around the concrete — not the other way around.

The Build Process

From Yard to Court

1

Site Evaluation

Ben visits your property to assess the lot. Grade, drainage, soil, access, and HOA or permit requirements are all evaluated before a proposal is written.

2

Subgrade Preparation

Excavation, grading, and compacted aggregate base. This is where the drainage slope is established — before a single yard of concrete is poured.

3

Form Work & Rebar

Forms are set to the exact court dimensions. Rebar grid is tied and properly spaced before pour day — no shortcuts on reinforcement.

4

Pour & Finish

Concrete is placed, screeded to grade, and finished with a medium broom texture designed to bond with acrylic sport court coating. Ben is on-site the entire pour.

5

Cure & Seal

Curing compound applied. Minimum 28-day cure period before surface coating. We build this into the project timeline from the start.

6

Acrylic Coat & Line Marking

Once the concrete has cured, we apply the acrylic sport court coating and paint the lines. Net posts, fencing, and gates can be added as part of the same project.

Court Specifications

What We Build To

Every pad is built to USA Pickleball construction standards. Here’s what that means in practice.

Dimensions

30 × 60 ft Minimum

Regulation play area is 20×44 ft. We recommend 30×60 ft total pad to include safety run-off on all sides. Tournament standard is 34×64 ft.

Drainage

2% Grade — One Direction

2 inches of drop per 10 linear feet. Established during subgrade prep, verified before pour. Water clears the surface fast — no pooling, no freeze-thaw damage at the coating bond.

Thickness

4″–5″ Slab

4-inch minimum for residential. 5-inch recommended for heavier use or clay-heavy soil. Mix design: 3,000–4,000 PSI with #4 rebar on 18″ centers.

Base

4″–6″ Compacted Aggregate

Crushed limestone base, properly compacted before forming. This is what keeps the slab stable over time — especially important on East Tennessee clay soils.

Finish

Medium Broom Texture

Not a smooth finish. Not a coarse driveway finish. A specific broom texture that provides traction and mechanical bond for acrylic sport court coating.

Cure Time

28 Days Before Coating

Concrete continues to cure and off-gas moisture after the pour. Surface coating applied before 28 days will delaminate. We schedule the cure window from day one.
